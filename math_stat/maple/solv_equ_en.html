Solving Equations

In this document, we show how to use Maple to solve (systems of) nonlinear equations and find extrema.

Roots of Polynomials

We start by finding the roots of the following polynomial.

> pe := x^3-x^2+5*x-6=0; spe := solve(pe,x);
\[ \begin{split} & pe := x^3 - x^2 + 5 x - 6 = 0 \\ & spe := \ldots \end{split} \]

We did not display the exact solutions given by Maple in the variable spe because it occupies several lines. The reader should run the commands above to get the long output.

The floating point representation (to \(10\) decimal places) of these roots is given by the following command.

> fspe:=evalf(spe);
\[ 1.157720809, -0.0788604043 + 2.275165423 I, -0.0788604043 - 2.275165423 I \]

To extract the values from this list, we may used the following commands. 

> x1 := fspe[1]; x2 := fspe[2]; x3 := fspe[3];
\[ \begin{split} & x1 := 1.157720809 \\ & x2 := -0.0788604043 + 2.275165423 I \\ & x3 := -0.0788604043 - 2.275165423 I \end{split} \]

Example: The goal is to find the points of intersection of the graphs of the following two functions.

> f1:=1/(x^2-8); f2:=x^2+4*x+2;
\[ \begin{split} & f1 := \frac{1}{x^2-8} \\ & f2 := x^2+4x+2 \end{split} \]

We first find the values of \(x\) where the graphs intersect.

> xs := evalf(solve(f1=f2,x));
\[ xs := 2.836678210, -.6331032006, -2.707918475, -3.495656534 \]

There are four real roots (see the figure below). We could instead have looked for the roots of the polynomial of degree four f2(x) / f1(x) - 1 = 0.

> plot1:=plot(f1(x),x=-4..4,-4..25,discont=true,color=red):
> plot2:=plot(f2(x),x=-4..4,-4..25,color=blue):
> with(plots):
> display({plot1,plot2});

To determine the coordinates of the points of intersection, we compute the values of f2 (we could as well have chosen f1) at each of the values of the list xs.

> f2f := x -> x^2+4*x+2:
> ys := seq(f2f(xs[i]), i=1..4);
\[ ys := 21.39345611, -.131593139, -1.498851433, .23698846 \]

We get the following four intersection points.

> seq([xs[i], ys[i]], i = 1 .. 4);
\[ \begin{split} & [2.836678210, 21.39345611], [-0.6331032006, -0.131593139], \\ & \qquad \qquad [-2.707918475, -1.498851433], [-3.495656534, 0.23698846] \end{split} \]

We have used the Maple command seq() to generate the list of values above.

Systems of Nonlinear Equations

Example: We want to find the solutions of the following system of two nonlinear equations.

> eq1 := 2*x^2-x*y+y^2 = 1; eq2 := x^2+y^2=1;
\[ \begin{split} & eq1 := 2x^2-xy+y^2 = 1 \\ & eq2 := x^2+y^2 = 1 \end{split} \]

We plot these two curves in the plan to locate approximately their points of intersection. We assume that \(-3 < x , y < 3\).

> with(plots):
> imp1 := implicitplot(eq1,x=-3..3,y=-3..3,grid=[40,40],color=red):
> imp2 := implicitplot(eq2,x=-3..3,y=-3..3,grid=[40,40],color=blue):
> display({imp1,imp2});

There seems to be four points of intersection (i.e. solutions of our system of linear equations). We first ask Maple to find them. 

> fsolve({eq1,eq2},{x,y});
\[ \{y = -1.000000000, x = 0.\} \]

Three of the solutions are missing. The function fsolve() uses an iterative method to find the solutions. Using solve() does not give more information. The reader should try it. To be able to find the other solutions, we need to provide a small area where the function fsolve() will have to look for a solution.

> fsolve({eq1,eq2},{x,y},{x=0.6..0.8,y=0.6..0.8});
\[ \{x = .7071067812, y = .7071067812 \} \]

We get another solution. The other solutions can be found using the same procedure or simply by using the reflective symmetry through the origin (i.e. \((x,y) \mapsto (-x,-y)\) ) of both equations. So the other solutions are just \(-1\) times the two solutions that we have found above.
