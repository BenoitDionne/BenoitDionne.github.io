Solving Equations

In this section, we show how to use Maple to solve systems of nonlinear equations and find extremum.

Roots of Polynomials

We start by finding the roots of a polynomial.

> pe := x^3-x^2+5*x-6=0; spe := solve(pe,x);
\[ \begin{split} & pe := x^3 - x^2 + 5 x - 6 = 0 \\ & spe := \ldots \end{split} \]

We did not write the exact solutions spe given by Maple because it occupies several lines. The reader should run the operations above to get the long output.

The floating point representation (to \(10\) decimal places) of these roots is given by the command

> fspe:=evalf(spe);
\[ 1.157720809, -0.0788604043 + 2.275165423 I, -0.0788604043 - 2.275165423 I \]

To extract the values from this list, we may used

> x1 := fspe[1]; x2 := fspe[2]; x3 := fspe[3];
\[ \begin{split} & x1 := 1.157720809 \\ & x2 := -0.0788604043 + 2.275165423 I \\ & x3 := -0.0788604043 - 2.275165423 I \end{split} \]

Example: Find the intersections of the graphs of the following two functions.

> f1:=1/(x^2-8); f2:=x^2+4*x+2;
\[ \begin{split} & f1 := \frac{1}{x^2-8} \\ & f2 := x^2+4x+2 \end{split} \]

We first find the values of \(x\) where the curves intersect.

> xs := evalf(solve(f1=f2,x));
\[ xs := 2.836678210, -.6331032006, -2.707918475, -3.495656534 \]

There are four real roots (see the graph below). Note that we could have solved instead the polynomial of degree four f2(x) / f1(x) - 1 = 0. 

> plot1:=plot(f1(x),x=-4..4,-4..25,discont=true,color=red):
> plot2:=plot(f2(x),x=-4..4,-4..25,color=blue):
> with(plots):
> display({plot1,plot2});

To determine the coordinates of the intersection points, we compute the values of f2 (we could as well have chosen f1) at each of the values of xs.

> f2f := x -> x^2+4*x+2:
> ys := seq(f2f(xs[i]), i=1..4);
\[ ys := 21.39345611, -.131593139, -1.498851433, .23698846 \]

to get the four intersection points:

> seq([xs[i], ys[i]], i = 1 .. 4);
\[ \begin{split} & [2.836678210, 21.39345611], [-0.6331032006, -0.131593139], \\ & \qquad \qquad [-2.707918475, -1.498851433], [-3.495656534, 0.23698846] \end{split} \]

We have used the Maple command seq() to generate a sequence of value.

Systems of Nonlinear Equations

Example: We want to find the solutions of the following system of two equations.

> eq1 := 2*x^2-x*y+y^2 = 1; eq2 := x^2+y^2=1;
\[ \begin{split} & eq1 := 2x^2-xy+y^2 = 1 \\ & eq2 := x^2+y^2 = 1 \end{split} \]

Let's plot these two curves in the plan to find approximately their intersections. We assume that \(-3 < x , y < 3\).

> with(plots):
> imp1 := implicitplot(eq1,x=-3..3,y=-3..3,grid=[40,40],color=red):
> imp2 := implicitplot(eq2,x=-3..3,y=-3..3,grid=[40,40],color=blue):
> display({imp1,imp2});

There seems to be four intersections (i.e. solutions of our system). Let see what maple gives.

> fsolve({eq1,eq2},{x,y});
\[ \{y = -1.000000000, x = 0.\} \]

Three of the solutions seems to be missing fsolve() probably uses a fixed point method to find a root. Using solve() will not give more information (try it). To be able to find the other solutions, we need to provide a small area where fsolve() should look into.

> fsolve({eq1,eq2},{x,y},{x=0.6..0.8,y=0.6..0.8});
\[ \{x = .7071067812, y = .7071067812 \} \]

We get another solution. The other solutions can be found using the same procedure or simply by using the reflective symmetry through the origin \((x,y) \to (-x,-y)\) of both equations. So the other solutions are just \(-1\) times the two solutions that we have found.
