Chaos and Fractals

The words chaos and fractal are now part of our ordinary vocabulary just as are the words table and chair. The words chaos and fractal can be found in the script of many films and television series produced since the 1980s. But beyond entertainment, there are beautiful and profound mathematical results associated to chaos and fractal geometry. The website starting with the document introduces some of the important ideas and results associated to these two mathematical concepts.

Many of the mathematical results stated in this website are accompanied by a proof or a sketch of the proof. References are given for the proofs of the results that are stated without proofs. Many of the sketch of proofs are in fact full proofs. They have been identified as sketch because they use mathematical results that are not included in this website.

Remark: The online LaTeX to mathml converter has been of enormous help to write the mathematical expression in mathml. However, we have not adopted their style files and scripts. Therefore, we are the only one to be blamed if the display of our mathematical expressions is not perfect. We have noted some little issues with the LaTeX to mathml converter above.

  • If the command \cal{} is used in a formula to produce a calligraphic letter, then all the following letters in the formula are also calligraphic. The command {\cal } must be used instead.
  • An expression of the form A^(2) is converted to A ( 2 ) instead of A ( 2 ) as it is expected in LaTeX.
  • The converter does not always handle correctly the commas when only integers are used. The comma in a vector like (1,2) is not converted to the expressions <mo separator="true">,</mo> but instead we get the expression <mn>1,2</mn>.
